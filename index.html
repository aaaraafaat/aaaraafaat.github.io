About me

I study machine perception that has to keep working when its input degrades, and autonomous systems that must act safely when it does not.

I flew for seventeen years in the Bangladesh Air Force as a fighter pilot and flying instructor. Much of that time was spent in poor weather, and much of the teaching was about one skill: knowing when a sense has stopped being reliable and changing to one that still is. I now want to build that skill into machines.

In September 2026 I completed an M.Sc. in Data Science and Machine Learning at State University of Bangladesh with a CGPA of 4.00 out of 4.00. My thesis tested whether a free, training-free fog score can warn an object detector that an image is hard. On real fog it cannot be trusted in one direction, and the paper reports that negative result in full.

I also served as a United Nations aviation safety officer in Darfur and taught Air Law and Airmanship as adjunct university faculty. Today I manage simulation and training-technology projects for the Air Force.

I am looking for a PhD position starting in Fall 2027 in robust perception, uncertainty and safe autonomy, with aerial robots as the first test case.

Research interests

  • Robust perception in degraded visual environments: fog, rain and low light.
  • Knowing when perception fails: calibrated confidence and failure detection for detectors.
  • Safe decisions after a failure: slow down, switch sensors or hand control to a person.
  • Simulation and human-in-the-loop training for safety-critical systems.
